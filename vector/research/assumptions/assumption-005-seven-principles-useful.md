# Assumption 005: Seven Principles Are Opinionated Enough to Be Useful

**Status:** hypothesis
**Risk:** Medium (Impact: Medium × Confidence: Medium)
**Personas:** All

## Statement

The Seven Principles of Zero Vector Design are specific enough to guide real decisions (which tool to use, how to structure a project, when to ship) without being so rigid that they block adoption by practitioners with different stacks, team sizes, or domains.

## Why This Matters

Principles that are too generic ("iterate often") provide no value. Principles that are too specific ("use React 19 with Vite") limit adoption. The Seven Principles sit at the methodology level — they need to survive tool changes while still being prescriptive enough to change behavior.

## Signals That Support It

- Practitioners citing specific principles in their decision-making ("I chose this because Principle 1: Work in the Medium")
- Principles applying across domains (web, mobile, hardware, content)
- Community not requesting significant rewording or additions
- Investiture doctrine files referencing principles as load-bearing constraints

## Signals That Would Invalidate It

- Community feedback that principles are "too vague" or "too obvious"
- Principles conflict with each other in practice (e.g., "Work in the Medium" vs "Boundaryless by Nature" in a team context)
- Practitioners adopt Zero Vector tools but ignore the principles
- Principles need constant reinterpretation for different contexts
