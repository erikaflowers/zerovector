# Interview Synthesis: Early Adopter Conversations

**Date range:** February–March 2026
**Participants:** 6 practitioners (3 designers, 2 engineer-designers, 1 design lead)
**Method:** Semi-structured interviews, 30–45 minutes each
**Interviewer:** Erika Flowers

## Key Themes

### 1. Handoff Fatigue Is Universal

Every participant described the handoff as their primary source of frustration, regardless of role. The language was remarkably consistent: "telephone game," "lossy translation," "signal degradation." One designer (P3) drew the same diagram unprompted that appears in The Box's sample data: arrows between roles, each arrow labeled "where we lose signal."

> "I don't need a better handoff tool. I need to stop handing off." — P2, engineer-designer

### 2. AI Adoption Is Ahead of AI Governance

All 6 participants use AI coding tools daily. None of them have a structured methodology for directing AI. They describe their practice as "prompting and hoping" or "generating and then fixing." The engineer-designers are further along — they've developed personal heuristics — but none have a shared team framework.

> "I've got my own system. My colleague has his. The codebase is two systems fighting." — P5, engineer-designer

### 3. The Word "Craft" Resonates Deeply

When asked what's missing from their current AI-assisted workflow, 4 of 6 participants used the word "craft" unprompted. They don't want to slow down — they want the speed to produce intentional output, not just functional output. The distinction between "it works" and "it's good" is important to them.

> "I can generate a landing page in ten minutes. But I can tell it's generated. Everyone can." — P1, designer

### 4. Progression Matters More Than Certification

When shown the Investiture progression model (Level 00–05), participants responded positively to the idea of a structured path — but explicitly rejected certification, badges, or credentials. They want capability, not proof of capability.

> "Don't make me take a test. Show me a project I can build that teaches me something real." — P4, designer

### 5. Community Is Desired but Fragile

Participants want to connect with others on the same journey but are skeptical of Discord servers, Slack groups, and forums. They've been burned by communities that devolve into beginner Q&A or self-promotion. They want a space where practitioners share real work, not tutorials.

> "If it turns into another 'look at my side project' feed, I'm out." — P6, design lead

## Implications for Zero Vector

1. The manifesto's confrontational tone aligns with practitioner frustration — they're ready for someone to name the problem directly
2. The curriculum should lead with building, not theory — participants lose patience with philosophy that isn't immediately applicable
3. AI governance (Investiture) is a real unmet need, not a theoretical one — practitioners are already feeling the debt
4. Community design should prioritize depth over breadth — curated, not open
5. The "one person + agents" positioning resonates with solo practitioners but needs proof points for leaders managing teams
