# Persona: The Technical Leader Governing AI

**ID:** persona-004
**Archetype:** CTO or VP Engineering managing AI adoption at scale
**Role:** CTO, VP Engineering, or CPO at a Series B–D company

## Context

His engineering team adopted AI tools 6–12 months ago. Productivity metrics went up. Code review quality went down. The team ships faster but the architecture is fragmenting — every engineer's AI generates slightly different patterns, naming conventions drift, and the codebase is accumulating debt at AI speed. He needs governance that enables velocity without becoming bureaucracy.

He's less interested in the design philosophy and more interested in the operational framework: how do you structure doctrine, enforce architecture, and coordinate AI agents across a team? Investiture's skill chain — preflight, manifest, audit, remediate — speaks directly to his problem.

## Goals

- Establish architectural governance that AI agents respect
- Maintain codebase coherence as AI-assisted development scales across the team
- Reduce the cost of AI-generated technical debt
- Have a repeatable audit → remediate → verify cycle

## Frustrations

- "We're shipping 3x faster and breaking 3x more. The net is zero."
- "Every developer's Copilot generates different patterns. We have five ways to do the same thing now."
- "I can't review AI output at the speed it's being generated. I need the system to enforce the rules."
- "Our architecture documentation is stale the moment it's written. Nobody reads it, including the AI."

## Quote

"I don't need AI to write more code. I need AI to write code that respects the architecture we already have."

## Behavioral Signals

- Tracks DORA metrics, cycle time, deployment frequency
- Has tried linting and automated review but finds it insufficient for architectural coherence
- Reads engineering blogs (Will Larson, Charity Majors, Martin Fowler)
- Evaluating structured AI governance frameworks
- Budget for tooling, skeptical of consultants
