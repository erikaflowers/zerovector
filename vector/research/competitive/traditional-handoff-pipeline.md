# Competitive Analysis: Traditional Design-to-Engineering Pipeline

**Type:** Incumbent paradigm (not a product)
**Threat Level:** Low (declining relevance) but deeply entrenched

## What It Is

The standard product development pipeline that has dominated for 20+ years: User Research → Information Architecture → Wireframes → Visual Design → Prototyping → Design Specs → Engineering → QA → Deploy. Each phase has its own specialists, tools, and deliverables. Handoffs between phases are the primary coordination mechanism.

## Strengths

- Well-understood roles and responsibilities — everyone knows their job
- Mature tooling at each phase (Figma, Jira, Confluence, etc.)
- Scalable to large teams — you can hire specialists and parallelize
- Risk management through sequential gates — each phase catches the previous one's errors

## Weaknesses

- Intent degrades at every handoff (estimated 30–70% signal loss across full pipeline)
- Coordination overhead consumes 30–50% of team capacity
- Cycle time is weeks to months, not hours to days
- The pipeline was designed for a world where building was expensive — AI makes building cheap, but the pipeline doesn't compress

## How Zero Vector Competes

Zero Vector doesn't compete on tooling (Figma vs Cursor) — it competes on structure. The pipeline is the problem, not the tools in the pipeline. By collapsing design and build into the same act (Principle 5), Zero Vector eliminates handoffs rather than optimizing them. The value prop is: "What if the person who understood the customer also built the product?"

## Key Differentiator

Zero Vector says the handoff is dead. The traditional pipeline says the handoff is improvable. These are incompatible positions — Zero Vector is a paradigm replacement, not an incremental improvement.
